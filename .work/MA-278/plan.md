## Risks
- A daily commitment started years back writes thousands of rows in its first pass, inside the exclusive transaction the app-start microtask opens, and `getActiveCommitmentDueDates` grows with rows, not queries.
- A browse years ahead writes every daily row through that month in one pass; See all then lists every one (MA-213 Decision B).
- MA-277 (#908) moves the settled-row loop at `commitment_housekeeping.helpers.ts:43-51`; whichever lands second rebases. A MA-277 preview that relied on the 64 default fails to compile under step 2's union, which is the intended signal.
- Step 4's coverage merge: a narrower pass landing after a wider one must not lower the recorded end; a wrong merge reruns passes, and `INSERT OR IGNORE` keeps them from writing duplicates.
## Self-assessment
Step 4 is the least sure. The current gate pairs one key with a request id and a generation check, and the three cases at `__tests__/commitment.store.test.ts:335-418` (old generation, a stale pass starting a snapshot early, reset) hold that pairing. Turning the key into a day and generation with a covered end, while two passes for different ends may be in flight, can break one of those cases if the request-id check is kept as is. The plan states the invariants and not the merge, so the implementer has to rework that guard against those three cases.
