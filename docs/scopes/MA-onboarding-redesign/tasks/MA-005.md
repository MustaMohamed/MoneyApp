# MA-005 — Onboarding resume: route resolver, persist-before-navigate, locked gestures

## Summary

Make the step the app has written down and the screen the user is looking at incapable of disagreeing — on relaunch, on a swipe back, and on a failed write.

## Details

This task was added at step 3. `spec.md` § *Architecture — Async and navigation ownership* specifies four behaviours that no task in the delivered breakdown owned, and two of them were being asserted by later tasks that had no way to satisfy them on their own. They are one concern, they are always reviewed together, and they are the mechanism behind business rule 2 — force-close during onboarding resumes from that step.

**None of this is a redesign.** The four onboarding routes still hold whatever bodies they hold when this lands. What changes is when the step is written, which route the app opens, and whether a gesture can get between the two.

### One — resuming lands on a step that is actually reachable

Today relaunch reads the persisted step and opens the matching route, full stop. It never asks whether that step makes sense given what is in the database. A session that was killed after the step advanced but before the account was written reopens on a screen that says an account was saved, above nothing.

The rule, from the spec: a persisted second step with at least one account resolves forward to the third; a persisted third or fourth step with no accounts resolves back to the second; anything else opens what was persisted. It is a pure function of the persisted step and the account count, and it is tested as one — every combination, including the ones that should pass straight through.

**The account snapshot is already loaded when this decision is made** — startup awaits it, and the router is not mounted until startup resolves. This has been checked; do not re-derive it, and do not add a load to make it true. What the plan *must* confirm is that the resolver cannot run against an empty snapshot in any startup path, including the retry after a startup failure, because a resolver that sees zero accounts sends a returning user back to the account form with their first account already saved. That is the one way this task can make things worse than they are today.

### Two — the step is persisted before the route changes, and the route is replaced

Every step transition writes the destination step first and then navigates, and it replaces the current route rather than stacking one. Today all three transitions push, so the back stack outlives the persisted step and a swipe can put the user on a screen the store no longer agrees with. This is a deliberate behaviour change, named as such in the spec.

**A failed step write is not silent.** If the persist rejects, the navigation does not happen, the user stays where they are with their input intact, the primary action returns to idle, and the failure appears in the footer's status track that MA-004 delivered. The button is the retry. This is defect class 1 in `.claude/rules/review.md` and it is the most repeated defect in this codebase — three of these transitions currently discard the rejection.

### Three — the onboarding stack does not accept gestures

Swipe-back is disabled for the whole onboarding stack. With routes being replaced there is usually nothing to go back to, but "usually" is what makes this the kind of thing that surfaces on someone's device and nowhere else. Back movement inside onboarding happens through the header's back affordance only.

### Four — a save cannot be undone by a route that has already gone

A save belongs to the form session that started it. A completion arriving after that session's route has been replaced must not navigate, must not close a route that replaced it, and must not write a step. Today nothing enforces this; with replace semantics and a slow write, it becomes reachable.

**Done when:** the resolver is a pure function with a test covering every persisted-step-and-account-count combination, including the pass-through cases; force-closing on each of the four steps and relaunching lands on the correct screen on the emulator, verified against what is actually in the database and including the killed-between-step-and-insert case; every step transition persists before navigating and replaces rather than pushes, under test; a forced failure of each step write leaves the user in place with the error in the status track and the action retryable, watched on the emulator at least once; swipe-back does nothing anywhere in onboarding on a real touch; a delayed completion arriving after its route was replaced changes nothing; **and the header's back affordance renders on N3 and N4 and performs the back movement this task defines, consistent with mockup frames E1 and F1.**

> **Step-3 gap, corrected at step 4 (2026-08-07, @tariq).** The final clause above is new. As delivered at step 3 this Done-when never required a back affordance to appear on N3 or N4 — only § *Three*'s prose mentions "the header's back affordance". MA-004 deliberately shipped both screens without one (`onboarding_header.tsx:50-52` renders a `w-9` spacer when `onBack` is absent) because deciding what that button *does* is this task's work; the hand-off was flagged three times inside MA-004 — plan decision 11, step-5 review finding 2 (`MA-004.md:480`), step-7 review (`MA-004.md:583`) — and recorded on issue #207. Mockup frames E1 (`assets/mockup.html:2004`) and F1 (`:2319`) both draw the chevron. Without this clause, MA-005 could be reviewed as complete with both screens silently missing it and nobody at fault.

## Plan

### Summary

- **One pure resolver, used in two places.** `resolveOnboardingStep(persistedStep, accountCount)` maps a persisted step plus an account count to the step that is actually reachable. It runs at the root redirect (resume) and again on every forward transition (so a destination can never be a step the data does not support). Twelve-row `test.each`, no router, no store.
- **Every navigation inside onboarding becomes persist-then-`replace`.** The three step transitions (N1→, N2→, N3→) and the three back actions (N2, N3, N4) all go through one runner that resolves the destination, awaits the persist, checks the session is still current, and only then replaces the route. `handleAddAnother` also becomes a `replace`, which is what makes "there is nothing on the stack" true rather than "usually true".
- **Every rejection becomes a rendered message in the status track MA-004 shipped.** Three handlers discard their rejection today (`welcome/index.tsx:31`, `add_account/index.tsx:57`, `more_accounts/index.tsx:33`); N4's is recorded in `useAsync`'s `isError` but rendered nowhere. After this task no onboarding handler can reject, and each one owns a `statusMessage` its shell renders. The CTA is the retry; no second button appears.
- **System back is consumed on N2, N3 and N4; N1 keeps today's exit.** One `BackHandler` subscription in `src/app/(onboarding)/_layout.tsx`, conditional on the active segment. `gestureEnabled: false` is added for iOS, where it is the only lever that exists; on Android the swipe *is* system back and the subscription is the lever. The N1 exemption was ruled at step 5 — see Decision 4.
- **N3 and N4 gain the back chevron the corrected Done-when requires.** N3 back persists N1 and replaces with Welcome; N4 back persists N3 and replaces with More accounts. Both are disabled while a write is in flight.
- **Session identity is a monotonic counter inside each screen's `.state.ts`.** Navigating away bumps it; a completion that lands on a stale session does not navigate, does not clear or set a message, and does not write a step.

Rows 5–7 and 10 are the four states rule 2 cannot describe because the persisted step is not reachable from the data; each resolves to the nearest step that is. `accountCount` is the non-archived snapshot count, since `AccountRepository.getAll()` is what `loadAccounts` publishes. **Read it as `useAccountStore.getState().accounts.length` at call time inside a transition, and as `useAccountStore(s => s.accounts.length)` only at `src/app/index.tsx`, where the redirect is a render** — see the correction under Decision 2 row 2 for why the difference is not cosmetic.

**Why the resolver runs on forward transitions and not only on resume.** @tariq presents this as MA-010's "N1 Continue returns to N3" falling out for free. Forward resolution is what keeps MA-005's own new affordance from opening a dead end. MA-010 inheriting a passing case is the side effect, not the reason.

#### Decision 1 — the empty-snapshot hazard, traced

**Finding: the resolver cannot observe a pre-load snapshot on any startup path, including the retry after a startup failure.** The trace, which @plan-reviewer should re-walk rather than take on trust:

1. `src/app/_layout.tsx:78-86` renders the root `<Stack>` **only** when `status === 'ready'`. Otherwise it renders `<StartupError>` or `null`. No route mounts — `src/app/index.tsx` included — before that.
2. `status` becomes `'ready'` only at `src/utils/use_layout_init.hook.ts:41` (`resolveReady(generation)`), which is after `await Promise.all([initOnboarding(), loadAccounts(), loadRate()])` at `:38`.
3. `loadAccounts` (`src/modules/accounts/store/account.store.ts:47-61`) publishes `{ accounts, hasLoaded: true }` at `:54` before resolving, and **rethrows** at `:59`. A rethrow rejects the `Promise.all`, so `use_layout_init.hook.ts:45` calls `rejectFatal` and the `<Stack>` never mounts. There is no branch where a failed load resolves ready with an empty array.
4. **The retry path.** `_layout.tsx:57` wires `retry` → `use_layout_init.hook.ts:53-55` → `start()` → `useAppReadyStore.begin()` (`src/store/ready.store.ts:29-33`). `begin()` sets `status: 'initializing'` and bumps `generation` — it does **not** clear `error` (`:31` writes only those two fields). So `_layout.tsx:68`'s `showStartupError = status === 'fatalError' || (status === 'initializing' && error !== null)` stays **true** for the whole retry, the `<Stack>` stays unmounted, and it remounts only when `resolveReady` sets `status: 'ready'` **and** `error: null` (`ready.store.ts:36`) — again strictly after `loadAccounts()` resolved. **This is the path the task singled out, and it is closed by `begin()` not clearing `error`.**

   **@plan-reviewer, round 1 — verified, and it is not an accident.** `__tests__/ready.store.test.ts:57-68` (`keeps the failure context mounted while a retry initializes`) asserts exactly this: `rejectFatal` then `begin()`, then `toMatchObject({ status: 'initializing', error })`. The behaviour is a pinned contract, not an omission, so a future "tidy" of `ready.store.ts:29-33` goes red rather than silently reopening the hazard. That test is the explicit dependency the task asked for. Do not touch `ready.store.ts` or that test in this diff, and if a later task needs to change `begin()`, that test is the one that must be argued with.
5. No other caller. `grep -rn "useAppReadyStore" src/` returns exactly `ready.store.ts` and `use_layout_init.hook.ts`; `begin()`/`reset()` have no other invocation, so there is no third path that can remount the router.
6. No account-store reset. `grep -rn "useAccountStore.getState().reset" src/` returns nothing — nothing empties the snapshot while onboarding is mounted.

**Therefore: do not re-derive the snapshot and do not add a load.** The resolver reads `accounts.length` from the already-published snapshot. It does **not** read `hasLoaded` as a gate — adding one would be a guard against a state proven unreachable, and it would fail open on the one case that matters (a genuine zero-account user would be indistinguishable from an unloaded one).

Residual risk, stated: a future task that mounts an onboarding route outside the ready gate reopens this. The mitigation is the trace above living in this file and step 6.4's test asserting the redirect reads the store rather than a local default.

#### Decision 3 — the shared runner and where session identity lives

Four new files, one deleted.

- **`src/modules/onboarding/domain/onboarding_route.ts`** (new) — `resolveOnboardingStep`, and `ONBOARDING_STEP_HREF: Record<OnboardingStep, Href>` moved verbatim from `src/app/index.tsx:7-12`. `Href` comes in as `import type`, so the file pulls no expo-router runtime and is importable from a plain `.ts` suite. Precedent for a `domain/` folder in a module: `src/modules/transactions/domain/transaction_amounts.ts`.
- **`src/modules/onboarding/domain/onboarding_transition.ts`** (new) — `runOnboardingTransition`, all dependencies injected so it is pure:

  ```text
  runOnboardingTransition({
    session,                                   // number returned by begin()
    api,                                       // { isCurrent, fail, settle, invalidate }
    navigate,                                  // (href: Href) => void — router.replace
    desiredStep,
    readAccountCount,                          // () => number — NOT a captured value
    persist,                                   // (resolve, isCurrent) => Promise<OnboardingStep>
    errorMessage,
  })
  ```
  Order, and it is the order the test asserts: `const resolved = await persist(resolve, isCurrent)` → on rejection `api.fail(session, errorMessage)` and **return without navigating** → on success `if (resolved === undefined || !api.isCurrent(session)) return;` → `api.invalidate()` → `navigate(ONBOARDING_STEP_HREF[resolved])`.

  **`resolve` is a thunk, not a precomputed step** (`@plan-reviewer, round 1`). `resolve()` is `resolveOnboardingStep(desiredStep, readAccountCount())`, and `readAccountCount` is `() => useAccountStore.getState().accounts.length` — read at call time, never captured at render. Three of the five transitions may call it before their first await, because nothing they do changes the count. **N2's save must call it after `addAccount` resolves**, which is why it is a thunk at all: see the correction under Decision 2 row 2.

  `persist` receives `isCurrent` because N2's persist contains **two** awaits and the guard has to sit between them, and it returns the step it actually wrote so the runner knows where to navigate. N2's body is exactly:

  ```text
  await addAccount(...)                        // count changes here
  if (!isCurrent()) return undefined;          // stale session: account stays, step is not written
  const resolved = resolve();                  // reads the POST-insert count → N3
  await setStep(resolved);
  return resolved;
  ```

  Returning `undefined` is the concrete meaning of "must not write a step" — a save whose route has gone leaves the account on disk and the step untouched, and the resolver's row 5 heals it on the next launch. The three back actions and N1's Continue have a single-await persist and return their `resolve()` directly; the add-more save branch writes no step and returns `OnboardingStep.N3` unchanged (its persisted step never moved).

- **`src/modules/onboarding/screens/onboarding/onboarding_transition.state.ts`** (new) — `createOnboardingTransitionState()`, shaped exactly like `src/modules/currency/screens/currency/currency.state.ts:27-38` (module factory + `createMoneyAppSelectors` + `create`). Reactive fields `{ statusMessage: string; busy: boolean }`; a closure `let session = 0` in the same position as `initGeneration` in `onboarding.store.ts:30` and `loadRequestId` in `account.store.ts:40`. Actions: `begin(): number | null` (returns `null` when `busy` — **this is the CTA re-entry guard, in one place**; otherwise `++session`, `set({ busy: true, statusMessage: '' })`), `isCurrent(s)`, `fail(s, msg)`, `settle(s)`, `invalidate()` (`++session`, `set(INITIAL_STATE)`), `reset()`.
- **`welcome/welcome.state.ts`, `add_account/add_account.state.ts`, `more_accounts/more_accounts.state.ts`** (new, one line each) — one instance per screen. **Not one shared instance**: a single slot written by three screens is audit L27 (`.claude/rules/state.md`, single-owner lookups), and during a `replace` the outgoing and incoming routes are briefly both mounted.
- **Deleted: `src/utils/onboarding_nav.ts` + `__tests__/onboarding_nav.test.ts`, and its entry in `jest.config.js` `collectCoverageFrom`.** `backOrReplace` has exactly two call sites (`add_account.hook.ts:82,90`), both rewritten here, and it is precisely the in-memory-history dependency `locked-design-2026-07-23.md:432` forbids. Leaving it in the tree invites re-adoption. Coverage is not in the pre-push chain, so this cannot break CI; `npm run test:coverage` sheds a file and its test together.

`statusMessage` is `''` when idle and is passed straight to `OnboardingShell`'s `statusMessage?: string` — `resolveStatusTrack` (`onboarding_shell.geometry.ts:79-93`) treats `''` as *no failure* and keeps the footnote, with `tone: 'idle'`, `accessibilityLiveRegion: 'polite'`. No `undefined` juggling, and no new prop: **the shell has `statusMessage` and no `statusTone`** — the tone is derived from message presence, so a failure is the only thing that can turn the track red. `spec.md:518`'s `statusTone` was never built; plan against the shipped signature (`onboarding_shell/index.tsx:11-22`).

> **@plan-reviewer, round 1 — verified, and the spec has been fixed rather than worked around.** The shipped props are `{ step, title?, onBack?, footnote, statusMessage?, cta, children }` (`onboarding_shell/index.tsx:11-22`); there is no `statusTone`, and `footnote` — which `spec.md:518` omitted entirely — is **required**, because the track is never empty. `resolveStatusTrack` derives the tone from message presence (`onboarding_shell.geometry.ts:79-93`), which is a stronger contract than a `statusTone` prop: a caller cannot paint the track red without a message, and cannot show a message without turning it red. #### Decision 4 — locking the stack, and the back affordance

**Two levers, because one platform each.**

- `src/app/(onboarding)/_layout.tsx` `screenOptions` gains `gestureEnabled: false`. Verified iOS-only: `react-native-screens/src/types.tsx:203-208` marks it `@platform ios`, and nothing under `react-native-screens/src/` reads it on Android. It is not decoration — it is the whole iOS answer.
- **Android: one `BackHandler` subscription in the same layout.** `app.json` sets `android.predictiveBackGestureEnabled: false` (verified), so the edge swipe *is* legacy system back; there is no separate app-level swipe to disable. Returning `true` from the handler consumes it. Dispatch walks `_backPressSubscriptions` in reverse and returns on the first `true` (`react-native/Libraries/Utilities/BackHandler.android.js:30-34`), falling through to `exitApp()` (`:36`) when every handler returns `false`. Subscribe in a `useEffect` **before** the `if (complete) return <Redirect …/>` early return, and return `subscription.remove()`.

  Read `useBackButton` carefully, because it changes what "do nothing" means: with every route replaced the onboarding stack is depth 1, `canGoBack()` is `false`, that handler returns `false`, and `BackHandler` falls through to `exitApp()` (`BackHandler.android.js:36`). **So without this subscription, `replace` would silently turn back on N2/N3/N4 from "go to the previous step" into "quit the app".** Consuming it is what prevents that, not a nicety.

  > **@plan-reviewer, round 1 — the conclusion holds but the stated reason was backwards.** The claim was "ours is registered after expo-router's container-level one, so ours runs first". By tree position it is the opposite: `useBackButton` is called from `expo-router/build/fork/NavigationContainer.js:40`, an **ancestor** of the route layouts, and React flushes effects child-first — so on a single commit the container would register *last* and therefore run *first* under LIFO. What actually puts ours last in the array is commit sequencing: `src/app/_layout.tsx:78` mounts the root `<Stack>` only when `status === 'ready'`, and `useAppInit` cannot reach `'ready'` on the first commit (`getDb` and `runMigrations` are awaited), so `(onboarding)/_layout.tsx` always mounts in a **later** commit than the container. Ours is genuinely last, and genuinely first to run.
  >
  > **And it does not matter either way, which is the load-bearing part.** At stack depth 1 expo-router's handler returns `false` and the loop continues to ours regardless of order. The only ordering-sensitive case is depth > 1, which decision 2's `handleAddAnother` → `replace` is precisely what eliminates. Keep both facts in the diff comment; a reader who checks only the tree position will otherwise "correct" this into a bug report.

- **N1 is exempt: back on the welcome screen still exits the app.** `@plan-reviewer's ruling, round 1 — @tariq deferred this to gate 3 and offered a one-line exemption; the exemption is the right default and is taken now rather than shipped for the user to discover.* The reason to consume back on N2/N3/N4 is that `replace` turns "back" into "quit" — a regression **this task introduces**. N1 has no such regression: back on N1 exits today, that is correct Android behaviour on a launch screen, and business rule 2 makes exiting safe (the app resumes on N1). Consuming it would make the first screen a new user ever sees the one screen they cannot leave, and § *Three*'s premise is that a gesture cannot **desynchronise the persisted step** — exiting the app desynchronises nothing.

  So the handler is conditional on the active route, not unconditional:

  ```text
  const segments = useSegments();
  useEffect(() => {
    const sub = BackHandler.addEventListener('hardwareBackPress', () =>
      segments[segments.length - 1] !== 'welcome',
    );
    return () => sub.remove();
  }, [segments]);
  ```
