# MA-005 — Onboarding resume: route resolver, persist-before-navigate, locked gestures

## Summary

Make the step the app has written down and the screen the user is looking at incapable of disagreeing — on relaunch, on a swipe back, and on a failed write.

## Details

This task was added at step 3. `spec.md` § *Architecture — Async and navigation ownership* specifies four behaviours that no task in the delivered breakdown owned, and two of them were being asserted by later tasks that had no way to satisfy them on their own. They are one concern, they are always reviewed together, and they are the mechanism behind business rule 2 — force-close during onboarding resumes from that step.

**None of this is a redesign.** The four onboarding routes still hold whatever bodies they hold when this lands. What changes is when the step is written, which route the app opens, and whether a gesture can get between the two.

### One — resuming lands on a step that is actually reachable

Today relaunch reads the persisted step and opens the matching route, full stop. It never asks whether that step makes sense given what is in the database. A session that was killed after the step advanced but before the account was written reopens on a screen that says an account was saved, above nothing.

The rule, from the spec: a persisted second step with at least one account resolves forward to the third; a persisted third or fourth step with no accounts resolves back to the second; anything else opens what was persisted. It is a pure function of the persisted step and the account count, and it is tested as one — every combination, including the ones that should pass straight through.

**The account snapshot is already loaded when this decision is made** — startup awaits it, and the router is not mounted until startup resolves. This has been checked; do not re-derive it, and do not add a load to make it true. What the plan *must* confirm is that the resolver cannot run against an empty snapshot in any startup path, including the retry after a startup failure, because a resolver that sees zero accounts sends a returning user back to the account form with their first account already saved. That is the one way this task can make things worse than they are today.

### Two — the step is persisted before the route changes, and the route is replaced

Every step transition writes the destination step first and then navigates, and it replaces the current route rather than stacking one. Today all three transitions push, so the back stack outlives the persisted step and a swipe can put the user on a screen the store no longer agrees with. This is a deliberate behaviour change, named as such in the spec.

**A failed step write is not silent.** If the persist rejects, the navigation does not happen, the user stays where they are with their input intact, the primary action returns to idle, and the failure appears in the footer's status track that MA-004 delivered. The button is the retry. This is defect class 1 in `.claude/rules/review.md` and it is the most repeated defect in this codebase — three of these transitions currently discard the rejection.

### Three — the onboarding stack does not accept gestures

Swipe-back is disabled for the whole onboarding stack. With routes being replaced there is usually nothing to go back to, but "usually" is what makes this the kind of thing that surfaces on someone's device and nowhere else. Back movement inside onboarding happens through the header's back affordance only.

### Four — a save cannot be undone by a route that has already gone

A save belongs to the form session that started it. A completion arriving after that session's route has been replaced must not navigate, must not close a route that replaced it, and must not write a step. Today nothing enforces this; with replace semantics and a slow write, it becomes reachable.

**Done when:** the resolver is a pure function with a test covering every persisted-step-and-account-count combination, including the pass-through cases; force-closing on each of the four steps and relaunching lands on the correct screen on the emulator, verified against what is actually in the database and including the killed-between-step-and-insert case; every step transition persists before navigating and replaces rather than pushes, under test; a forced failure of each step write leaves the user in place with the error in the status track and the action retryable, watched on the emulator at least once; swipe-back does nothing anywhere in onboarding on a real touch; and a delayed completion arriving after its route was replaced changes nothing.
