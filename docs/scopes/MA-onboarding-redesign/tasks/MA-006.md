# MA-006 — 32-colour bottom sheet replaces the swatch rows

## Summary

Build the colour picker the design specifies — thirty-two colours, all visible at once in a bottom sheet — and adopt it in the two Accounts-module screens where an account colour is chosen today.

## Details

An account colour is currently picked from a cramped wrapping row of twelve circles, written three different ways across the app. The approved replacement is a single sheet showing all thirty-two colours at once, as a chart: eight columns, two rows per tone block, with **column position meaning the same family in both blocks** — the rich and soft tones of one family sit directly above and below each other. Reference frames are mockup D1 and D2; the sheet's anatomy, labels and copy are in `spec.md` § *Product & UX — The 32-colour sheet*.

**Nothing scrolls.** Fitting all thirty-two on one fixed-size surface is the point: the picker reads as a complete chart rather than a list you have to hunt through.

**Selecting a colour must not move anything.** The selected cell gains a ring and a check mark, both drawn inside padding the cell always has, so no tile resizes and the grid never reflows. The check mark's colour is chosen per swatch so it stays legible on that specific colour — light swatches get a dark tick. Assuming white is the defect frame D2 exists to demonstrate. Both the tick colour and the family name come from the palette MA-001 published; nothing here re-derives either.

**Opening the sheet stages a choice; only the sheet's own confirm button commits it.** Closing by the close control or by swiping down discards the staged colour and leaves the form's value untouched. This matters because the sheet is opened from inside a form and a discarded pick must not dirty the draft.

**Each swatch announces its family and tone**, and whether it is selected. Colour is never the only signal.

**The row that opens it** shows the currently chosen colour, its family and tone in words, and a chevron — mockup C1's *Account colour* row. The words come from looking the stored value up in the palette, so a colour saved before this scope still names itself correctly.

**Scope of adoption in this task: the Accounts module only** — the add-account screen reached from Settings, and the account detail screen, which today share one twelve-swatch component. That component has exactly these two consumers and goes away with them. The onboarding copy is left alone here; it is deleted in MA-008 when onboarding adopts the shared form, and reaching into onboarding now would spread one task across two modules. The categories colour picker is a different feature with a different palette and is not touched.

**The account detail screen has a second, quieter problem to fix while you are in it:** the colour it falls back to when an account has none is read from the divergent twelve-value list, not from the list it draws swatches from. The two happen to agree on that one entry today, which is why nobody noticed. The default and the choices must come from the same source once this lands.

**Two things to check while building rather than after:** the sheet's built-in close control is a small icon button and may not clear a 44-point target without help; and a scrollable inside a sheet has to come from the sheet's own library or it will not scroll at all. This sheet is not scrollable, but the surrounding form is.

**Done when:** all thirty-two colours are reachable and selectable from both Accounts-module entry points; selecting one changes only the ring and the tick, with no cell resizing or grid reflow; discarding leaves the previous colour intact and confirming persists the new one through a save, confirmed against what is actually in the database; a test asserts the grid's column ordering pairs each family's tones and that every swatch has a distinct accessible name; an account with no stored colour opens on a default that is in the palette; and the twelve-swatch component the two screens shared is gone rather than left behind.
