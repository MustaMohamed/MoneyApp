# MA-010 — N1 Welcome

## Summary

Rebuild the first screen a new user ever sees: the oversized headline composition, the two currency choices that each state their own consequence, and a continue action that persists before it navigates.

## Details

### Inherited from MA-002 — read before planning

MA-002 shipped the **gradient** treatment and left three facts this task will hit. They are recorded here because "does not revisit the decision, does not re-measure it" below correctly steers the planner away from MA-002's file — which would otherwise mean these are found by tripping over them.

1. **A caller's `style={{ marginTop: … }}` silently overwrites `DisplayHeadline`'s negative inset** (`src/components/ui/display_headline.tsx:31`). The component's `style?: StyleProp<ViewStyle>` merges last and carries no doc comment. The inset is −0.238 em and is what keeps the descender of "Finally" from clipping, so overwriting it looks like a font bug rather than a layout one. **This task is the first consumer.** Space the headline with a wrapper or a sibling, not with `marginTop` on the component.
2. **At `font_scale 2.0` the headline clips horizontally.** Not a defect — the gated range is 0.85–1.3 and 2.0 was recorded as out-of-band evidence. But N1 is the app's first screen, so decide deliberately what happens at accessibility sizes above 1.3 rather than discovering it at gate 3.
3. **Native vs linear scaling diverge by 5.52 dp at `fontScale` 1.3.** `react-native-svg` has no dynamic-type support, so `DisplayHeadline` multiplies by `useWindowDimensions().fontScale` itself — linear, where the platform's own text scaling is not. MA-002's open question 5 deferred this to MA-010 **by name**. It never gated MA-002's verdict because both treatments diverge from the native reference in opposite directions.

Also carried: **gate 3 must confirm the headline is announced "Finally clear." once, as a heading.** The AVD had no TalkBack, so the label is proven and the *role* is pinned only by a unit assertion — `uiautomator` has no heading attribute. "Heading" is the load-bearing word.

Reference frames are mockup B1 through B5; the composition, copy and selection language are in `spec.md` § *Product & UX — N1 Welcome*.

**The composition is scale, not decoration.** A giant ghost numeral behind the content, a short gold rule, a small eyebrow, and an oversized left-aligned headline. No illustration, no card stack, no centred block. The headline's second line is the treatment MA-002 settled — this task consumes whatever that spike shipped and does not revisit the decision, does not re-measure it, and does not branch on which one it was.

**Behind everything sits a two-hue ambient wash.** It is the screen's base canvas, not a material on top of a surface — no blur, no translucency, no white edge. It belongs to the shell, behind the scrolling content, not inside it. This is the app's very first screen and its cold-start budget is under two seconds on a mid-range Android device; the wash is the one thing on this screen that could cost measurably, and the emulator run is where that gets checked rather than assumed.

**The currency choice is a set of two radio rows, not a tab bar.** Each row states what choosing it actually means — one says every total is shown in Egyptian pounds, the other says totals convert using the rate you save. That framing is the settled answer to what choosing dollars promises: **a display consequence, not an architectural one** (`scope.md`, decision 1). Egyptian pounds are pre-selected, as the business rules require. Selection is marked by colour and border, never by a glow, and the row is exactly as tall selected as unselected.

**Below it, a trust line** stating that data never leaves the device and there is no cloud account to create.

**Four blocks rise in on mount**, staggered, once, opacity and a small lift only — and not at all when the user has reduced motion enabled. This is one of the three animations in the entire flow (`spec.md` § *Motion budget*); nothing else on this screen moves.

**Continue persists both the chosen currency and the destination step before it navigates**, and it replaces the route rather than stacking one. That ordering and the replace are MA-005's contract — this screen adopts it, it does not re-invent it. If either write fails the user stays on this screen with their selection intact, the button returns to idle, and the failure appears in the footer's status track — the button itself is the retry. Nothing re-animates on a failure, because nothing was re-selected.

**This screen has no empty state and no skeleton.** A currency is pre-selected and startup has already loaded everything the route needs before it mounts. Frame B5 is a written statement rather than a drawn frame for exactly that reason: the four-state floor exists to catch forgotten states, not to license invented ones.

**When an account already exists** — the user came back here from the more-accounts step to review their currency — continuing returns them to that step rather than to the account form. This is the one navigation edge the mockup does not draw; `spec.md` § *Open questions* item 3 confirms it, and MA-005's resolver is where the rule lives. Changing the currency here does not change any account's own currency.

**Done when:** the screen matches B1 and B2 on the emulator, including that switching currency moves nothing but colour; the busy and failed states match B3 and B4, with a forced failure of the currency write showing the error in the status track, the selection preserved, the button retrying and nothing re-animating; the persisted step is correct after a force-close on this screen; the headline is announced correctly by a screen reader; the four blocks animate once on mount and not at all with reduce-motion on, watched with the setting toggled; a first-paint measurement with the ambient wash present is recorded in this file as a number; and tests cover the persist-before-navigate ordering, the failure path, and the return-to-more-accounts case.

## Plan

### Summary

- **The behaviour this screen is famous for is already shipped and already tested.** MA-005 put the persist-before-navigate runner, the `router.replace`, the session guard and the resolver call into `welcome.hook.ts:27-47`, and `__tests__/screens/onboarding_welcome.hook.test.ts:76-116` already asserts the persisted step and the `replace` href (`:76-83`), the return-to-more-accounts case (`:85-93`) and both failure paths (`:95-105` currency write, `:107-116` step write, each asserting no navigation, the status message and `busy === false`). The **strict ordering** assertion — persist runs before navigate — lives one level down at `__tests__/onboarding_transition.test.ts:17` (`'calls persist before navigate, in that order'`), which is the runner N1 delegates to; the hook suite pins N1 to that runner. Together they discharge the clause. *(Corrected at plan review: the original text placed the ordering assertion inside the hook suite, where it is only implied by the two failure paths.)* **`welcome.hook.ts` is not modified by this task and neither suite is rewritten** — keeping them green unchanged is the evidence that the contract was adopted rather than re-invented. Three of the four Done-when test requirements are discharged by them; the heading-role pin is the fourth.
- This task is therefore a **presentation rebuild**: the Broadsheet composition (ghost `01`, gold rule, eyebrow, two-line 42 px headline), the two-hue ambient wash on the shell behind the scroll view, two currency radio rows that each state their consequence, a trust row, four one-shot entry animations, and the `Continue` CTA wired to the hook that already exists.
- `DisplayHeadline` (MA-002, gradient treatment, `src/components/ui/display_headline.tsx`) gets its **first consumer**. It is spaced by a wrapper, never by `style={{ marginTop }}`, and it gains one additive optional prop — `maxFontScale` — which is how the two accessibility questions MA-002 deferred get answered.
- **Line 1 (`Your money.`, RN text) is drawn from the same geometry resolver as line 2 (`Finally clear.`, SVG)** with `allowFontScaling={false}`, so both lines scale linearly and identically. That is the answer to the 5.52 dp native-vs-linear divergence: it is removed from where it is visible (two adjacent lines of one headline) rather than accepted.
- **Both headline lines stop growing above `fontScale` 1.3.** Everything else on the screen — body copy, currency rows, trust line, CTA, footnote — keeps scaling to 2.0. The headline is the one element that can neither wrap nor reflow, and MA-002 measured it clipping at 2.0.
- The wash is **two `RadialGradient`s in one static `Svg`**, mounted by the shell behind a transparent `Screen`, with no blur, no animation and no new dependency. Its cost is measured on the emulator as an A/B against the identical build with one prop removed, and the number is written into this file.
- Net SVG count on N1 is roughly flat: this task **deletes** `GeoIllustration` (one `Svg`, one `RadialGradient`, 5 shapes — N1 is its only consumer) and adds the wash (one `Svg`, two `RadialGradient`s) plus the headline's `Svg`.

### Decisions

Recorded here because each one is a call a reviewer should be able to disagree with explicitly.

**D1 — The headline is spaced by its container, never by `style` on the component.**
`DisplayHeadline` merges the caller's `style` last (`display_headline.tsx:31`), so a caller's `marginTop` overwrites the −0.238 em `topInset` that keeps the descender of "Finally" inside the box. The two headline lines live in one `<View>` with **no `gap` and no margin between them** — the negative inset is exactly what makes the SVG line occupy one 1.05 line box under line 1 (at 42 px: `boxHeight` 54, `topInset` −10, net 44 = `round(42 × 1.05)`). Spacing above the headline is carried by the *block's* container, i.e. by the gold rule's sibling margin. No `style` prop is passed to `DisplayHeadline` at all.

**D2 — Above `fontScale` 1.3 the headline stops growing. Nothing else on the screen is capped.**
`resolveDisplayHeadlineGeometry` gains an optional third parameter `maxFontScale`; N1 passes `DISPLAY_HEADLINE_MAX_FONT_SCALE = 1.3`, the top of this app's gated range. Above it the drawn size freezes while body copy, both currency rows, the trust line, the CTA and the footnote continue to scale — the copy a user must actually read is untouched. The headline is an SVG text node: it cannot wrap, cannot reflow, and MA-002 recorded it clipping horizontally at 2.0, which loses the word "clear." outright. Freezing is strictly better than clipping, and the string is still announced in full by the `accessibilityLabel` the component already sets.
**Cost, stated:** a user at `fontScale` 2.0 sees a headline drawn at 1.3× above body copy drawn at 2.0×, so the brand line is proportionally smaller than the mockup's ratio. Accepted deliberately. Existing MA-002 assertions call the two-argument form and are unaffected — the parameter defaults to no clamp.

**D3 — The 5.52 dp native-vs-linear divergence is removed at the seam where it shows, not accepted.**
MA-002 open question 5, deferred to this task by name. `DisplayHeadline` multiplies by `fontScale` linearly because `react-native-svg` has no dynamic-type support; RN's own text scaling on Android 14+ is non-linear, so an ordinary `Text` at the same nominal size lands 5.52 dp away at `fontScale` 1.3. On N1 those two scaling laws would sit on **adjacent lines of one headline**, which is where 5.5 dp is unmistakable.
Fix: line 1 renders with `allowFontScaling={false}` and an explicit size from a new `resolveDisplayHeadlineTextStyle(...)` that shares `resolveDisplayHeadlineGeometry`'s arithmetic, so the two lines are matched *by construction* and a unit test asserts `fontSize` equality at 0.85 / 1 / 1.15 / 1.3 / 2. The residual divergence against the *rest* of the screen (eyebrow 10.5 px, body 14 px, both natively scaled) is left as-is: a few dp between elements of different size and weight is not perceivable as a mismatch, and correcting it would mean disabling font scaling on copy people actually read. **Answered, with a bounded residual, not deferred again.**

**D4 — The wash is a sibling *outside* `Screen`'s padded box, and `Screen` goes transparent.**
`OnboardingShell` gains an optional `background?: ReactNode` (same shape as its existing `cta: ReactNode`) and renders `<View style={{flex:1}} className="bg-background">{background}<Screen style={{ backgroundColor: Colors.shared.transparent }}>…</Screen></View>`.
Why not simply put the wash inside `Screen`: RN positions an absolutely-positioned child against its parent's **padding box**, and `Screen` applies the safe-area insets as padding (`screen.tsx:41-44`). An `absoluteFill` wash inside it would start below the status-bar inset and leave a visible horizontal seam across the top of the app's first screen under Android's edge-to-edge. Hoisting the wash one level up and pushing the background colour onto the host View costs one extra `View` node on all four onboarding routes and changes nothing about how N2–N4 look — they omit `background` and paint the same `bg-background`, one level up. The inline `backgroundColor` beats the compiled `bg-background` class because `style` wins over `className` in RN (the mechanism MA-009 documented at `account_type_tile.tsx:29-33`), and this is the most visible thing on the screen, so the emulator run settles it in one screenshot.

**Two things added at plan review, both verified in the tree:**
- `Screen` bakes `bg-background` into its *own* `className` (`screen.tsx:48`, `cn('bg-background', className)`) — it is not passed in by the shell. So the failure mode if the inline style loses is not a seam, it is **the wash invisible entirely** (it sits under an opaque `Screen`). Scenario 1 catches that in the same screenshot. **Deterministic fallback if it does lose:** pass `className="bg-transparent"` to `Screen` instead of the inline style — `cn` is `tailwind-merge`-backed (`heroui-native/src/helpers/external/utils/cn.ts:52`), so `cn('bg-background', 'bg-transparent')` de-conflicts to `bg-transparent` by construction. Use that and record which one shipped; do not stack both.
- `OnboardingHeader` and `OnboardingFooter` paint **no background of their own** (`onboarding_header.tsx:31`, `onboarding_footer.tsx:25` — border classes only), so after this change the wash shows through both. Behind the header that is the intent (the mockup's `.hdr`, line 274-278, has no background either, and the wash is on `.device`). Behind the footer it is a small deviation — the mockup's `.foot` paints `var(--background)` opaquely (`mockup.html:318-320`) — but at the transcribed geometry neither ellipse reaches the footer band (teal's lower edge is `0.34h + ms(340)` ≈ 627 of 844 dp against a footer top ≈ 700), so expect no visible difference and do **not** add a background to the footer to chase it; that would change all four routes.

Build: one `Svg` at `width`/`height` from `useWindowDimensions()`, two `Rect`s filled with `RadialGradient`s at `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`, transcribed from `mockup.html:428-433` (`.aurora`) — gold `Colors.dark.gold` @ 0.15 → 0 at offset 0.6, ellipse `ms(470) × ms(320)` centred at `(0.10w, −0.06h)`; teal `AcctTokens.nile.rich` (`#2D7D6E`, the mockup's `rgba(45,125,110,.13)`) @ 0.13 → 0 at 0.62, ellipse `ms(420) × ms(340)` centred at `(1.02w, 0.34h)`. No blur, no `expo-blur`, no translucent panel, no animation, no `useEffect`.

**D5 — The two currency consequence sentences render full-strength, not `--content-secondary`.**
The mockup draws them `.t-cap dim` (`mockup.html:1059`, `:1067` — corrected at plan review from `:1054`/`:1062`, which are the section question and a closing tag). `spec.md:122` states the rule every task in this scope inherits: *anything a user must read is full-strength; the muted colour is confined to genuinely redundant labels* — and `scope.md` decision 2 names **these exact two sentences** as the ones that contradict "handled by rule", because decision 1 (what choosing USD promises) rests entirely on them. MA-009 resolved the identical conflict the same way (impl review D4, `account_type_tile.tsx:116-125`). The intro body paragraph follows the same rule for the same reason. Hierarchy is carried by size and weight — 16 px Sora semibold label over 12 px Inter consequence — which `scope.md:77` already records as the accepted side effect. The muted token survives on this screen only in the header's `Setup` micro-label, which MA-004 already shipped. **This is applying a standing scope ruling, not re-opening Marcus's design;** if @plan-reviewer disagrees, this is the line to argue.

**D6 — Row height is derived from the symbol box, and the mockup's own CSS gives 74 where its caption and the spec both say 76.**
`CURRENCY_ROW_MIN_HEIGHT = Math.max(Size.shieldBox + Spacing.sm * 2 + Size.hairline * 2, TouchSize.min)` → 48 + 24 + 2 = **74** at the 390 pt reference. `mockup.html:441-447` (`.opt`) computes the same 74: `box-sizing: border-box` is global (`:209`), `min-height: calc(var(--size-dialog-button) + var(--space-xl))` = 44 + 24 = 68, and that floor is overridden by the content — the 48 pt `--size-shield-box` symbol column (`:129`) plus 12 pt `--space-sm` padding each side (`:82`) plus the 1 pt border. The text column (16 px label + 4 px `mt4` + 17 px caption line ≈ 44) never governs. **74 is what the approved frame actually draws.**

**Corrected at plan review — the "76" is not only the spec's prose.** It is written on the approved mockup itself, in B1's own caption: *"radio semantics, 76 pt target"* (`mockup.html:1083`), and `spec.md:63,124` transcribe it from there. So this is a **recorded, deliberate 2 dp deviation from an annotation in the approved design**, not the correction of a typo — most likely the caption reused the type tile's 76 (`.tile`, `:496`, `44 + --space-xxl 32 = 76`, which the spec also states at `:73`). It is taken as team-decided because: the *drawn* frame is the visual authority and it draws 74; both numbers clear the 44 pt touch floor `spec.md:124` is actually asserting; and forcing 76 means authoring a literal in place of a derivation, for the reason `STATUS_TRACK_LINE_HEIGHT` is derived (`onboarding_shell.geometry.ts:16-23`). **@dev records the 74 in `## Emulator verification` explicitly so gate 3 reads it as a decision rather than measuring a row that disagrees with the spec.**

**D7 — Selection changes colour only, and the row style object is shared literally between both states.**
One module-level `CURRENCY_ROW_STYLE` carrying `minHeight`, `borderWidth: 1`, `borderRadius`, `padding`, `flex` metrics; the render-prop's `isSelected` branch may set **only** `borderColor` and the fill class (`border-accent bg-accent/15` selected — `bg-accent/15` is literally the mockup's `--accent-soft`, `color-mix(… 15%, transparent)`, `mockup.html:198` — versus `border-border bg-surface` unselected). `borderWidth` is identical in both states, which is what makes the geometry unmovable; a unit test asserts the shared object contains no colour key at all. No glow, no halo, no shadow, no scale. HeroUI's `Radio` supplies the circle and its own selected fill (`heroui-native/src/styles/components/radio.css:43-45`), which is the "HeroUI's own language" the spec asks for; its indicator-thumb scale is a primitive-level transition, not a fourth animation, and it snaps under reduce-motion via Reanimated's own `ReduceMotion.System` default (`react-native-reanimated/src/animation/util.ts:150-155`).

**D8 — The gold rule and ghost numeral ship in the shell folder, not in the welcome folder.**
`mockup.html` draws `.b-rule` on B1, E1–E5 and F1–F9, and `.b-ghost` on B1 and E1–E5 — i.e. MA-011 and MA-012 consume both. They are ~15 lines of `View` between them, so putting them in `onboarding_shell/onboarding_broadsheet.tsx` now costs nothing and puts them where the next two tasks will already be reading. This is a within-scope task-ordering call (team-decided), not a new abstraction: the shared element already exists in the approved design.

**D9 — The ghost numeral does not scroll, and it is allowed to bleed.**
`mockup.html:404-408` positions it `top:-30 right:-16` relative to the content body, so it overlaps the progress-rail band and the right edge, clipped only by `.device`'s own `overflow:hidden`. Inside a `ScrollView` that bleed is clipped at the content edge instead, so the numeral renders as an absolutely-positioned, non-scrolling sibling of `ScreenScroll` inside the shell's content viewport — the same reading as the wash: it is canvas, not content. **If the emulator screenshot shows it clipped at the viewport's top edge, that is Android Fabric's overflow handling, not a mistake:** set `top: 0`, record it in `## Emulator verification`, and do **not** add `overflow: 'visible'` to the shell's viewport `View`, which would change all four routes.

**D10 — `welcome.hook.ts` keeps its `useState`.**
The module screen anatomy says hooks hold no `useState`, and 19 of the tree's 45 `*.hook.ts` files carry one today, including all four onboarding hooks. Moving `selected` into a Zustand slot would churn a shipped, MA-005-reviewed hook and its seven passing tests to comply with a rule the tree does not enforce, for a screen-local radio selection. Left alone deliberately — flagged so it reads as a decision rather than an oversight.

**D11 — The CTA loses its entry animation; the motion budget is four blocks, not five.**
`welcome.anim.ts:12` animates the CTA today. `spec.md` § *Motion budget* and `mockup.html:1036-1077` (`rise-1`…`rise-4`) name exactly four blocks, all inside the content viewport: headline block · body column · currency block · trust row. The footer is a fixed track and does not enter. One fewer animation on the first screen.

**D12 — Dead surface orphaned by this task is deleted in this task.**
`src/components/geo_illustration/` and `Strings.o1Headline` / `o1Subtext` / `o1Cta` / `n1CurrencyLabel` / `n1CurrencyNote` have exactly one consumer between them — the screen this task rewrites (verified: `grep -rn` over `src/` and `__tests__/` returns only `welcome/index.tsx` and the declarations). Leaving them behind would hand step 9 a `debt:quality` issue for a deletion only this diff can justify.
